Two ways to say stop

`ask` rules and a `deny` default make a policy that can be tightened one
rule at a time. Neither covers the case where an operator wants it all to
stop: getting back to "nothing is allowed" by hand means being sure about
every rule rather than about one, and doing it while something is going
wrong means editing TOML and waiting for a watcher.

Two commands, and the difference is what they outlive.

`agent-iap acl reset` deletes every `[[acl]]` rule and sets `acl_default`
to `deny`. Both halves, because either alone still grants everything: rules
under an `allow` default, or one surviving `allow` rule over a `deny` one.
It asks first, refuses rather than assuming with no terminal to ask at, and
prints the rules it removed by name — that being the last place they exist.
`R` on the console's rules pane is the same edit.

`agent-iap run --lockdown` denies every request without touching the file.
It decides before the rule list, so no rule can get in front of it, and it
sits outside everything a reload swaps — a kill switch the agent's own
`acl add` could lift is not one. `L` toggles it on the console, the header
says LOCKDOWN in red while it is on, the audit log records the refusals
against `<lockdown>`, and `/status` reports it read-only: the switch belongs
on the operator's terminal, not behind an admin token.

And the bell. An `ask` that nobody answers denies, which made the queue's
usefulness depend on somebody happening to be looking at it — a console in
a background window cannot be seen, but the terminal it is in can be heard.
A parked request writes BEL, and what the terminal does with that is the
operator's setting: a sound, a flashing window, a tmux flag, a notification.
A burst is one ring rather than a dozen, since a dozen beeps is a sound
people turn off. Off with `--no-bell`, `IAP_NO_BELL` or `approval_bell`,
and never rung at a terminal nobody is watching.

`clipboard`'s /dev/tty writer moves to `term`, since the bell wants the
same handle for the same reason: neither is program output, and under the
console ratatui owns stdout.
